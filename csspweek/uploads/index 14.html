Chapter 02 / Who we are

Being CSSP — shaped by being a Rajah.

To be CSSP is to belong to the College of Social Sciences and Philosophy: a home for people who study one another, who ask why the world is the way it is, and who refuse to look away. And to be a Rajah is the name we carry into that work — not a title given once, but an identity chosen again every year. A Rajah leads with questions, serves with conviction, and stands with the community. Below is what that name asks of us.

The Wooden Sword

Conviction

The Rajah's blade is wooden on purpose — carried to defend ideas, not to wound. It is the courage to argue for what is right, to raise a hand in a room that has gone quiet, and to hold a position because the evidence, and the people, demand it.

What it means

The Putong

Honor & Service

The putong — the woven crown of our pagpuputong — is never worn for oneself. It is placed on a Rajah by the community to say: you are trusted now, so serve. Honor here is measured in what you give back, not in the crown you wear.

What it means

The Shield

Solidarity

No Rajah stands alone. The shield is the batchmate who shows up, the org that carries you, the college that becomes a second home. We rise as an us — and the story only grows because someone before us made room in it.

What it means

Chapter 03 / How it began

Our first pagpuputong.

Before there were twenty-one years, there was one afternoon. A wooden sword raised, a putong woven from what the community could gather, and a name given: Rajah. Our first pagpuputong wasn't a ceremony we watched — it was a welcome we felt, a rite of passage that said you are one of us now. Every year since, we crown again. Here, it's your turn.
